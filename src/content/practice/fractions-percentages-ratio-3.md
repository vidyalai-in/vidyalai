This set tests ratios and proportion: sharing in a ratio, working through one unit, and spotting whether two quantities go up together or one goes down as the other goes up.

## The methods

- **Dividing in a ratio** a : b: add the parts, find one part, then multiply. ₹300 in 2 : 3 gives 5 parts of ₹60, so ₹120 and ₹180.
- **Unitary method:** find the value for **one** first, then multiply.
- **Direct proportion:** both go up together (more pens, more cost). The ratio stays the same.
- **Inverse proportion:** one goes up, the other goes down (more workers, fewer days). The **product** stays the same: workers × days = total work.
- **Joining two ratios:** make the shared term equal using the LCM.

## Worked example

*A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 6 : 7. Find A : B : C.*

B is 4 in one ratio and 6 in the other. LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. Multiply the first ratio by 3 (9 : 12) and the second by 2 (12 : 14). So A : B : C = **9 : 12 : 14**.

## Mixtures

Find how much of each ingredient there is in litres, not in ratio parts. When you add only water, the milk does not change, so work from the milk. When you mix two jugs, choose an amount that splits neatly into both jugs' parts.

## Common trap

Ask "if one goes up, does the other go up or down?" before you multiply. Using direct proportion on an inverse question is the most common mistake in these sets.
