| What changes | Mirror image | Water image |
|---|---|---|
| Where is the mirror? | On the right of the object | Below it, like a lake |
| What swaps? | Left and right | Top and bottom |
| Order of letters | Reversed | Unchanged |
| Each letter | Flipped left to right | Upside down |

A **half turn** (turning the page upside down) is different from both. It reverses the order *and* turns every letter upside down. It is what you get if you take the water image of a mirror image.

## Symmetric letters

Some letters do not change in a mirror: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y. Some do not change in water: B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X. Use these lists to check options quickly, and look at the letters that *do* change, such as R, F, G, J, 4 and 7.

## Worked example

The mirror image of **RAM** reads M, A, R from left to right, with the R facing left. M and A look the same as before, so only the R gives it away. Writing MAR in normal letters is the classic wrong answer.

## Clocks

In a mirror, 12 and 6 stay put and 3 and 9 swap. A quick rule: **mirror time = 11:60 − actual time**. For 3:20 that gives 8:40. In a water image, 12 and 6 swap and 3 and 9 stay where they are.

## Tip

Always check two things: the **order** of the characters, and the **direction** each one faces. Most wrong options get one of the two right.
