This set tests how well you know what words mean. A **synonym** is a word with the same or nearly the same meaning. An **antonym** is a word with the opposite meaning.

## How to choose

1. Make up a short sentence with the word in capitals, such as "The *rapid* train reached Pune early."
2. Try each option in its place. A synonym keeps the meaning. An antonym turns it around.
3. Read the question again before you mark. "Closest in meaning" wants a synonym. "Most nearly opposite" wants an antonym.

## Worked example

*Most nearly opposite to CAUTIOUS: Careful, Reckless, Quiet, Clever.*

A cautious person thinks before acting and avoids danger. *Careful* means the same, so it is a synonym, not the answer. *Quiet* and *clever* are unrelated. A **reckless** person rushes into danger without thinking, so that is the opposite.

## Traps to watch for

| Trap | Example |
|---|---|
| A synonym placed in an antonym question | *Rare* offered as the opposite of *scarce* |
| A word that looks or sounds similar | *Obvious* for *obstinate*, *dessert* for *desert* |
| A word that goes with it but means something else | *Costly* for *scarce* |
| Something in between, not opposite | *Translucent* for *transparent* |

## Prefixes help

*Im-*, *in-*, *un-*, *dis-* and *mis-* often turn a word into its opposite: *partial* and *impartial*, *honest* and *dishonest*. If you know the root word, you can often work out the meaning.
