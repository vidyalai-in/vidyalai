This set tests percentages: finding a percent of something, measuring a change, and using percents for marks, profit, loss and discount.

## The rules

- **Percent of a number:** 15% of 360 = 15/100 × 360. Build it from easy pieces: 10%, 5%, 1%.
- **Fraction to percent:** multiply by 100. 3/4 = 75%, 1/8 = 12.5%.
- **Percent change** = change ÷ **original** value × 100.
- **Profit % and loss %** are always on the **cost price**.
- **Discount %** is always on the **marked price**. Selling price = marked price − discount.

## Multipliers make it quick

| Change | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| up 20% | 1.2 |
| down 20% | 0.8 |
| up 5% | 1.05 |
| down 15% | 0.85 |

Two changes in a row multiply: up 10% then down 10% is 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99, a 1% fall overall.

## Worked example

*A pen is sold for ₹90 at a loss of 10%. What did it cost?*

A 10% loss means the selling price is 90% of the cost. So 90% of the cost = ₹90, 1% is ₹1, and the cost price is **₹100**. Note that 10% of ₹90 is ₹9, but ₹99 is wrong: the loss is 10% of the cost, not of the selling price.

## Common trap

When you work backwards from a final amount, do not add or take away the percent from that final amount. Write "final = multiplier × original" and divide.
