This set tests two skills: finding the rule that changes one figure into another and using it again, and spotting the one figure that does not belong.

## Analogies: A is to B as C is to ?

1. Compare the first two figures part by part. What moved, turned, changed colour, or was added?
2. Say the rule in words, for example "a quarter turn clockwise" or "one more side and one more dot".
3. Apply exactly that rule to the third figure, and check each part of your answer.

Common rules:

| Rule | What you see |
|---|---|
| Turn | Every part goes round the centre by the same amount |
| Mirror image | Left and right swap, top and bottom stay |
| Water image | Top and bottom swap, left and right stay |
| Colour swap | Black parts become white, white parts become black |
| Counting | One more side, one more dot, one more line |

## Odd one out: turned or flipped?

When three figures are the same drawing turned around, the odd one is often its **mirror image**. A turn never changes the order of the parts going clockwise. A mirror image reverses it.

**Worked example.** A square has a dot in one corner and a short line on the next side, going clockwise. However you turn it, going clockwise from the dot you still meet the line next. In a mirror image you meet the line going anticlockwise instead.

## Common trap

A half turn and a water image both move the top to the bottom. Check left and right too: a half turn also swaps them, a water image does not.
