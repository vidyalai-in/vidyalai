In an odd-one-out question, three items share a rule and one does not. Your job is to find the rule, not just a difference.

## Method

1. Look for the **strongest** rule: one that the three others all clearly share. Common rules are what kind of thing it is (fruit, sense organ, unit of length), what it does, or a number or letter pattern.
2. Check that exactly one item breaks your rule.
3. Then ask: does any *other* rule pick out a different item? If so, the stronger, more meaningful rule wins. A shared meaning beats something on the surface, like the first letter or the length of a word.

## Rules to try

| Words | Numbers | Letter groups |
|---|---|---|
| kind of thing | prime or not | gaps between letters |
| what it is used for | square, cube | opposite letters (sum 27) |
| what it is made of | multiples (of 3, 7, 11, ...) | vowels and consonants |
| letters in order | digit sums, middle digit | letters in order |

## Worked example

*Which is the odd one out: Eagle, Sparrow, Crow, Bat?*

Eagle, sparrow and crow are birds. A bat flies too, but it is a mammal: it has fur and feeds its babies milk. **Bat** is the odd one out. "Things that fly" is not the rule, since all four fly.

## Trap

Do not stop at the first difference you see. In *Lion, Tiger, Leopard, Cow*, lion and leopard both start with L, but that is a surface link. Lion, tiger and leopard are wild cats, so **Cow** is the odd one out. Always look for what the three share.
