      The subject is *each*, which is singular, so the verb is singular: **has**.

This set tests the small words that make a sentence correct: verb forms, articles, prepositions, pronouns and joining words.

## Rules to know

| Topic | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | Find the real subject. Ignore words after *of*, *as well as*, *along with*. | One of the girls **is** late. |
| Each, every | Singular verb. | Every student **has** a book. |
| Past perfect | For the earlier of two past actions. | The bus **had left** before we arrived. |
| If (future) | Present in the *if* part, *will* in the other. | If you **call**, I will come. |
| a / an | Depends on the first **sound**, not letter. | **an** hour, **a** uniform |
| since / for | *since* + a point in time, *for* + a length of time. | since 2022, for two years |
| Pronouns | Subject: I, he, she, we. Object: me, him, her, us. | **She and I** met him. |

## Spotting an error

Read the whole sentence, then test one part at a time. Ask: does the verb agree with its subject? Is the tense right for the time words? Is the right preposition used? If every part is correct, choose "No error".

## Worked example

*"Neither Ravi nor his friends ___ the answer." (know / knows)*

With *neither ... nor*, the verb agrees with the subject **closer** to it. That is *friends*, which is plural, so the answer is **know**.

## Common trap

The noun just before the verb is often not the subject. In "The box of mangoes is heavy", the subject is *box*, so the verb is *is*, even though *mangoes* is plural and sits right next to it.
