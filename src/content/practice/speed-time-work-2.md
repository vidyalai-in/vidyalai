This set tests the two classic special cases of speed and time: trains, which have a length, and boats, which are helped or slowed by the river.

## Trains: what distance does it cover?

| The train passes | Distance covered |
|---|---|
| a pole, a signal or a standing person | its own length |
| a platform, bridge or tunnel | its length + the platform's length |
| another train | the two lengths added |

For two trains (or a train and a walking person), use the **relative speed**: add the speeds if they move in opposite directions, subtract if they move the same way. Change km/h to m/s with × 5/18, since lengths are in metres and times in seconds.

## Boats and streams

- Downstream (with the river): boat + stream.
- Upstream (against the river): boat − stream.
- Boat speed = (downstream + upstream) ÷ 2.
- Stream speed = (downstream − upstream) ÷ 2.

## Worked example

*A 150 m train at 54 km/h crosses a 250 m bridge. How long does it take?*

54 km/h = 54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s. Distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m. Time = 400 ÷ 15 = **26 2/3 seconds**.

## Common trap

Forgetting the train's own length when it crosses a platform is the most common mistake. Picture the engine entering the platform and the last coach leaving it: the engine moves the platform length *and* the train length.
