    topic: Punctuation
    difficulty: 1
    q: "Which sentence is punctuated correctly?"
    options:
      - '"Where are you going." asked Ravi.'
      - '"Where are you going?" asked Ravi.'
      - '"Where are you going" asked Ravi?'
      - '"Where are you going?", asked Ravi.'
    answer: B
    explanation: |
      Ravi asked a question, so his words end with a question mark, and it belongs **inside** the quotation marks.
      No comma is added after a question mark, so the answer is **"Where are you going?" asked Ravi.** A full stop is wrong because the words are a question.

  - id: rg3-passive-letter
    topic: Active and passive
    difficulty: 1
    q: "Change into the passive voice: Meera wrote a letter."
    options:
      - "A letter was written by Meera."
      - "A letter is written by Meera."
      - "A letter was wrote by Meera."
      - "A letter has been written by Meera."
    answer: A
    explanation: |
      *Wrote* is simple past, so the passive is *was* + past participle: **A letter was written by Meera.**
      "is written" changes the tense to present, and "was wrote" uses the wrong form of the verb.

  - id: rg3-indirect-tired
    topic: Direct and indirect
    difficulty: 1
    q: 'Change into indirect speech: Ravi said, "I am tired today."'
    options:
      - "Ravi said that I was tired that day."
      - "Ravi said me that he was tired that day."
      - "Ravi told that he was tired that day."
      - "Ravi said that he was tired that day."
    answer: D
    explanation: |
      *I* becomes *he* (Ravi is talking about himself), *am* becomes *was*, and *today* becomes *that day*: **Ravi said that he was tired that day.**
      *Told* needs a person after it (*told me*), and *said* does not take one (*said me* is wrong).

  - id: rg3-comma-list
    topic: Punctuation
    difficulty: 1
    q: "Which sentence uses commas correctly?"
    options:
      - "Asha bought rice, dal, and, sugar at the shop."
      - "Asha bought rice dal and sugar, at the shop."
      - "Asha bought rice, dal and sugar at the shop."
      - "Asha, bought rice, dal and sugar at the shop."
    answer: C
    explanation: |
      In a list, a comma separates the items, and the last two items are joined by *and*: **Asha bought rice, dal and sugar at the shop.**

  - id: rg3-passive-bridge

    options:
      - "The bridge is painted by the workers."
      - "The bridge was being painted by the workers."
      - "The bridge has been painting by the workers."
      - "The bridge is being painted by the workers."
    answer: D
    explanation: |
      *Are painting* is present continuous. Its passive is *is/are being* + past participle: **The bridge is being painted by the workers.**

  - id: rg3-indirect-question
    topic: Direct and indirect
    difficulty: 2
    q: 'Change into indirect speech: The teacher asked Kabir, "Where do you live?"'
    options:
      - "The teacher asked Kabir where did he live."
      - "The teacher asked Kabir where he lived."
      - "The teacher asked Kabir where do you live."
      - "The teacher asked Kabir that where he lived."
    answer: B
    explanation: |
      In a reported question, the words go back to normal statement order (*he lived*, not *did he live*), there is no question mark, and *that* is not added before the question word.
      So the answer is **The teacher asked Kabir where he lived.**

  - id: rg3-indirect-command
    topic: Direct and indirect
    difficulty: 2
    q: 'Change into indirect speech: Mother said to Arjun, "Close the window."'
    options:
      - "Mother said Arjun to close the window."
      - "Mother told to Arjun to close the window."
      - "Mother told Arjun to close the window."
      - "Mother told Arjun that close the window."
    answer: C
    explanation: |
      An order is reported with *told* (or *asked*, *ordered*) + the person + *to* + verb: **Mother told Arjun to close the window.**

  - id: rg3-order-roti
    topic: Sentence order
    difficulty: 2
    q: "Put the sentences in the right order to make a paragraph.\nP: Then she mixed the flour with water and kneaded it into a soft dough.\nQ: Finally, she rolled out round rotis and cooked them on a hot tava.\nR: Neha's grandmother first sifted the wheat flour into a large bowl.\nS: After the dough had rested for ten minutes, she made small balls from it."
    options: ["RPSQ", "RSPQ", "PRSQ", "RPQS"]
    answer: A
    explanation: |
      R names the person and says *first*. P (*then*) makes the dough. S needs the dough to exist already. Q says *finally*.
      So the order is **RPSQ**. PRSQ starts with *Then*, which cannot open a paragraph.

  - id: rg3-apostrophe
    topic: Punctuation

    q: "Which sentence uses the apostrophe correctly?"
    options:
      - "The childrens' books were kept on the top shelf."
      - "The childrens books were kept on the top shelf."
      - "The children's books were kept on the top shelf."
      - "The childrens's books were kept on the top shelf."
    answer: C
    explanation: |
      *Children* is already plural and does not end in *s*, so it takes **'s**, like *men's* and *women's*: **The children's books were kept on the top shelf.**

  - id: rg3-passive-who
    topic: Active and passive
    difficulty: 3
    q: "Change into the passive voice: Who broke the window?"
    options:
      - "Who was the window broken?"
      - "By whom the window was broken?"
      - "By whom was the window broke?"
      - "By whom was the window broken?"
    answer: D
    explanation: |
      *Who* (the doer) becomes *by whom*, and since it is still a question, *was* comes before the subject: **By whom was the window broken?**
      "By whom the window was broken?" keeps statement order in a question, and "broke" is the past tense, not the past participle.

  - id: rg3-order-ghats
    topic: Sentence order
    difficulty: 3
    q: "Put the sentences in the right order to make a paragraph.\nP: These rains fill the rivers that flow east across the Deccan.\nQ: When the monsoon winds blow in from the Arabian Sea in June, they hit these hills.\nR: The Western Ghats are a long chain of hills near India's west coast.\nS: Forced to rise, the moist air cools and drops heavy rain on the slopes."
    options: ["RSQP", "RQSP", "QRSP", "RQPS"]
    answer: B
    explanation: |
      R introduces the hills. Q's *these hills* points back to R. S's *the moist air* is the monsoon wind from Q, rising over the hills. P's *these rains* points back to the rain in S.
      So the order is **RQSP**. In RSQP, "the moist air" appears before any wind has been mentioned.

  - id: rg3-indirect-yesterday
    topic: Direct and indirect
    difficulty: 3
    q: 'Change into indirect speech: Priya said to me, "I met your brother yesterday."'
    options:
      - "Priya told me that she had met my brother the day before."
      - "Priya told me that she had met your brother the day before."
      - "Priya told me that I had met my brother the day before."
      - "Priya told me that she has met my brother the day before."
    answer: A
    explanation: |
      *I* (Priya) becomes *she*; *your* (spoken to me) becomes *my*; *met* goes back to *had met*; *yesterday* becomes *the day before*.
      So the answer is **Priya told me that she had met my brother the day before.** "your brother" forgets that the brother is mine, and "has met" cannot be used with a past time like *the day before*.

This set tests how sentences are put together: punctuation, active and passive voice, reported speech, and the order of sentences in a paragraph.

## Active and passive

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject. Use a form of *be* + the past participle, in the **same tense**.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Asha cleans the room. | The room is cleaned by Asha. |
| Asha cleaned the room. | The room was cleaned by Asha. |
| Asha is cleaning the room. | The room is being cleaned by Asha. |
| Asha has cleaned the room. | The room has been cleaned by Asha. |

## Direct to indirect speech

When the reporting verb is in the past (*said*, *asked*):

- the tense goes one step back: *am* → *was*, *met* → *had met*, *will* → *would*;
- pronouns change to fit who is speaking and to whom;
- time and place words change: *today* → *that day*, *yesterday* → *the day before*, *here* → *there*;
- questions go back to statement order: "Where is she?" → where she was;
- orders use *to*: "Sit down." → told him to sit down.

## Worked example

*Farhan said to me, "I will help you tomorrow."* → Farhan said that he would help me the next day.

## Putting sentences in order

Look for the sentence that can **start** a paragraph: it names the person or thing. Words like *then*, *finally*, *these*, *it* and *they* point back to something already said, so they cannot come first. Follow the chain of those links.

## Common trap

In punctuation questions, check every option for capitals, question marks and apostrophes, not just the first mistake you notice.
