      In the hills of Meghalaya, in north-east India, some bridges are not built. They are grown. The Khasi and Jaintia people who live there have found a clever way to cross the fast streams that rush down the hills in the monsoon.

      They guide the roots of the rubber fig tree across a stream. To point the young roots in the right direction, they often use a frame of bamboo or the hollow trunk of an areca palm. Over the years, the roots grow longer and thicker and twist together, until they form a strong, living bridge.

      The work needs great patience. A bridge can take ten to fifteen years, sometimes more, before it is strong enough to walk on. Yet the wait is worth it. This part of India gets some of the heaviest rain in the world, and a bridge made of wood or rope soon rots in the damp air. A root bridge does the opposite. Because it is alive, it keeps growing, and it becomes stronger as it gets older. Some of these bridges are believed to be more than a hundred years old, and villagers still cross them every day to reach their fields and markets.

This set tests how well you read: finding the main idea, picking out details, working out word meanings and reading between the lines.

## Kinds of question

| Question | What to do |
|---|---|
| Main idea or title | Pick the answer that covers the **whole** passage, not one detail. |
| Detail | Find the exact sentence. The answer is written there, often in other words. |
| Word in context | Read the sentences around the word. Put each option in the word's place and see which one fits. |
| Inference | The answer is not written, but the passage gives strong clues. Pick the one with the most support. |

## How to read

1. Read the whole passage once, quickly, for the main idea.
2. Read the questions.
3. Go back to the passage to find each answer. Do not answer from memory or from what you already know.

## Worked example

*"The old bus groaned up the hill. Halfway up, it stopped, and the driver asked everyone to get out and walk." Why did the driver ask them to walk?*

The passage does not say why, but "groaned" and "stopped" halfway up a hill are strong clues. The best inference is that the bus could not climb the hill with everyone on it. "The driver wanted them to enjoy the view" has no support.

## Common trap

An option can be true in real life and still be wrong, because the passage does not say it. Another option can use words copied from the passage but give them a different meaning. Always check the answer against the text.
