---

This set tests whether you can spot the rule that changes a figure from one step to the next, and use it to draw the next step.

## What can change

Look at one part of the figure at a time and ask what happens to it from step to step:

| Change | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Turning | By how much? 45° is half a right angle, 90° a quarter turn. Clockwise or anticlockwise? |
| Moving | Which places does it visit, corners only, or corners and the middles of sides? How many places per step? |
| Adding | Does one line, side or dot get added each time? |
| Shading | Does the colour switch black, white, black? Or go through three colours? |

## Worked example

A triangle points up, then right, then down. It is turning a quarter turn clockwise each step, so it will point left next. If it is also black, white, black, the next one is white.

## Two rules at once

Many series have **two parts that change in different ways**, for example an arrow that turns while a dot moves around the corners. Follow each part separately, then put the answers together. Check that the option you pick gets both parts right.

## Growing steps

Sometimes the step itself grows: a dot moves 1 place, then 2, then 3. Write the jumps down and look for the pattern in them.

## Common trap

After four quarter turns, a figure is back where it started. Count the turns instead of guessing from the picture.
