This set tests whether you can picture a folded, punched sheet of paper when it is opened out.

## The rule

When you open a fold, every hole gets a **partner on the other side of the fold line, at the same distance from it**. The fold line acts like a mirror. A hole close to the fold gets a partner close to the fold; a hole near an edge gets a partner near the opposite edge.

## Open the folds in reverse order

Undo the **last fold first**. Each time you undo a fold, the number of holes doubles (unless a hole sits exactly on a fold line).

| Folds | Layers | Holes from one punch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 8 | 8 |

So first count the holes, and cross out every option with the wrong number.

## Worked example

A sheet is folded in half from top to bottom, then from left to right, so only the bottom right quarter is left. A hole is punched near the bottom right corner of this small square. Undo the left-right fold: a second hole appears near the bottom left corner. Undo the top-bottom fold: two more appear near the top corners. The answer has 4 holes, one near each corner.

## Diagonal folds

For a fold along a diagonal, the partner hole swaps its distances: a hole 2 cm from the left edge and 5 cm from the top gets a partner 5 cm from the left and 2 cm from the top (for the diagonal from the top left corner).

## Common trap

**Sliding** a hole across the fold instead of **flipping** it. A slide keeps the spacing the same, so a hole near the fold ends up near the far edge. That is wrong.
