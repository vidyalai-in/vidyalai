## Issues found
- "bf16 base with LoRA needs about 16 GB for weights plus activations": 8B × 2 bytes ≈ 16 GB is the frozen weights alone. Activations at 4K tokens add roughly 25 GB without checkpointing, plus 4-6 GB of logits for a 128-152K vocabulary.
- "fits on a 24 GB card with gradient checkpointing": only with ≤2K-token micro-batches and a fused loss.
- "5K examples at batch 32 is about 156 steps per epoch": the train split is 4,500 → 141 steps.
- "On 250 test items, the CI is about ±3-5 points": true only near 85-95% accuracy; ±6.2 at 50%.
- Base model example (Llama-3-8B, 2024) dated as of October 2026; newer small open models exist (Qwen3.5-9B, Feb 2026; Gemma 4, 2026).
- "a slice of MMLU": MMLU is saturated for current models.
- QLoRA "10-30% slower" unsourced.

## Changes made
- Worked example switched to Qwen3-8B (dense, Apache 2.0, 8.19B params; config verified on Hugging Face), with Llama 3.1 8B alongside; noted Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma 4 use hybrid/multimodal architectures, so recompute from config.
- LoRA params: formula `r × Σ(d_in + d_out)`; Qwen3-8B 75,776 per layer × 16 × 36 = 43.6M (0.53%); Llama 3.1 8B 41.9M (0.52%), confirming the original "~40M, 0.5%".
- Memory budget table (4,096-token micro-batch): bf16 no checkpointing ≈ 46-48 GB; with checkpointing ≈ 23-25 GB (≈20 GB with fused CE); QLoRA ≈ 13-15 GB (≈9-10 GB). QLoRA weights ≈ 6.1 GB for Qwen3-8B (embeddings and lm_head stay bf16), 5.7 GB for Llama 3.1 8B. 70B QLoRA ≈ 40 GB NF4 weights + 3.3 GB LoRA state (207M params) → one 80 GB GPU.
- Wall clock from FLOPs: 4-6 × N per token, 4.4-6.6 × 10^16 FLOPs/epoch, 2-4 min/epoch on an H100 at 30-40% MFU.
- Steps fixed to 141/epoch (280-420 total; 140-210 at batch 64); added LoRA's large-batch sensitivity from LoRA Without Regret.
- 250-item CI recomputed (±3.7 / ±5.7 / ±6.2 at 90/70/50%).
- MMLU → MMLU-Pro or similar. QLoRA slowdown framed as a planning figure to measure.
- Full fine-tuning row: 16 bytes/param ≈ 130 GB for 8B before activations.
- Added `## Thinking at scale` (the original had none).
